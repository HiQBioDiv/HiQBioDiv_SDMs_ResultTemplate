HiQBioDiv results: template

1 Terminology and acronyms

This chapter defines the principal abbreviations and methodological terms used throughout the workflow and species accounts.

Acronyms and abbreviations

AUC - Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve
A threshold-independent discrimination metric describing the ability of a model to rank occurrence locations above background locations. AUC ranges from 0.5, indicating discrimination no better than random expectation, to 1, indicating perfect discrimination. In presence-background models, it is sensitive to the extent and composition of the background region (Lobo et al. 2008)
CBI - Continuous Boyce Index
A presence-only model-evaluation metric assessing whether observed occurrence locations are concentrated in areas receiving higher predicted suitability. Values range from -1 to 1. Positive values indicate agreement between model predictions and occurrence data, values near 0 indicate performance close to random expectation, and negative values indicate an inverse relationship (Hirzel et al. 2006).
EGV - Ecogeographical variable
An environmental predictor used in habitat-suitability modelling. EGVs used in this project describe climate, topography, soils, hydrology, land use and land cover, Earth-observation products, landscape structure, and other environmental characteristics. Many EGVs are available at several spatial scales.
HSM - Habitat suitability model/map
Depending on context, refers to either the fitted model describing environmental suitability or its spatial prediction. Where ambiguity is possible, the terms habitat suitability model and habitat suitability map are written in full.
LULC - Land use and land cover
Classification of the terrestrial surface according to land use and land-cover characteristics. LULC information is used both as environmental information and to evaluate changes occurring between the year of a species observation and 2024.
ORMTP - Minimum-training-presence omission rate
The proportion of evaluation occurrences receiving predictions below the minimum suitability predicted for any training occurrence. Training omission is zero by definition. Values above zero indicate that some evaluation occurrences receive suitability values below all training occurrences (Kass et al. 2021).
OR10 - 10% omission rate
The proportion of evaluation occurrences falling below the suitability threshold defined by the 10th percentile of predictions at training occurrences. Values around 0.10 are expected by construction for training data; substantially larger evaluation values indicate poorer transfer to withheld data (Kass et al. 2021).
SDM - Species distribution model
A statistical model relating species occurrence information to environmental predictors to estimate the relative suitability of locations for a species. In this book, SDMs are presence-background Maxent models unless stated otherwise.
TSS - True Skill Statistic
A threshold-dependent discrimination metric calculated as sensitivity + specificity - 1. TSS ranges from -1 to 1, with higher values indicating better discrimination. Independent-test TSS is calculated using a threshold selected from the training data and then applied unchanged to the independent test data (Allouche et al. 2006).
VIF - Variance Inflation Factor
A diagnostic measure of multicollinearity among predictor variables. VIF quantifies how strongly the variance of a predictor’s estimated coefficient is inflated because that predictor can be explained by other predictors in the model. Larger values indicate stronger multicollinearity. In this workflow, predictors with VIF > 10 were removed during the initial screening step (Dormann et al. 2013a).

Key terminology

Analysis cell
The basic spatial unit used for modelling. The principal analysis grid has a resolution of 100 m × 100 m, corresponding to an area of 1 ha.
Association
In this book, a descriptive relationship between predicted habitat suitability and a land-use, habitat, Natura 2000, or conservation-regime category. Association does not imply a causal effect or behavioural habitat selection.
Apparent population
A relative proxy derived from the amount of habitat suitability retained along the Zonation priority ranking. Because continuous habitat suitability, rather than abundance, is used as the biodiversity feature, this quantity should not be interpreted as an estimate of actual population size.
Background location
A location sampled from the accessible modelling region and used to characterise environmental conditions available to the species. Background locations are not assumed to represent confirmed species absences.
Bias layer / effort-bias layer
A raster describing spatial variation in observation or sampling effort. It is used as a probability surface when sampling background locations so that background sampling better reflects the spatial structure of species-recording effort.
Cloglog suitability
Habitat suitability expressed using the complementary log-log transformation of Maxent predictions. Values range from 0 to 1 and are used as the principal continuous habitat-suitability output in this workflow.
Conservation-priority rank
The continuous rank produced by Zonation, ranging from low-ranking to highest-ranking cells. Values approaching 1 identify cells with the greatest relative contribution to the specified single-species conservation objective.
Continuous relative suitability index
An index describing whether predicted habitat suitability is disproportionately concentrated within a land-use, habitat, or conservation category relative to the area occupied by that category. It is calculated as the proportion of total predicted suitability occurring in a category divided by the proportion of the total area represented by that category. A value of 1 indicates proportional representation, values above 1 indicate disproportionately high predicted suitability, and values below 1 indicate disproportionately low predicted suitability.
Cross-validation
Evaluation in which subsets of the calibration data are withheld from model fitting and used for validation. Spatial cross-validation is used where possible so that training and validation observations are geographically separated.
Extinction-risk proxy
A prioritisation-derived measure calculated from the proportion of apparent population remaining along the Zonation ranking. It represents relative loss risk under the prioritisation framework and must not be interpreted as a demographic extinction probability or an IUCN extinction-risk estimate.
Independent test set
Occurrence and background data withheld before model tuning and cross-validation and used only for final model evaluation. Models designated TrainVal do not contain an independent test set, whereas TrainValTest models do.
Marginal response
The predicted relationship between habitat suitability and one environmental predictor while the remaining predictors are held at fixed values, here generally their arithmetic means. Marginal responses are used for ecological interpretation but should be interpreted cautiously when predictors are correlated.
Maxent
A presence-background modelling approach used here to estimate relative habitat suitability from species occurrences and environmental predictors. Model complexity is controlled through feature classes and regularisation.
Null model
A model generated after randomisation of occurrence information and used to establish the level of model performance expected by chance. Empirical evaluation metrics are compared with distributions obtained from repeated null models.
Permutation importance
A measure of predictor importance obtained by randomly permuting the values of a predictor and quantifying the resulting decrease in model performance. Larger decreases indicate a greater contribution of that predictor to model predictions.
Preference
This term is avoided where possible in the interpretation of model-derived habitat associations because predicted habitat suitability does not directly measure behavioural selection. Where retained in internal variable names or historical outputs, preference refers to the continuous relative suitability index rather than demonstrated habitat preference.
Presence location
A spatially referenced record indicating that the focal species was observed at a location. Presence records are subjected to reliability, spatial, temporal, and, where applicable, LULC-change filtering before modelling.
Primary priority area
The highest-priority portion of the landscape identified from the single-species Zonation performance curves. In this workflow, its threshold is selected near the point where the apparent-population and extinction-risk-proxy curves are closest.
Secondary priority area
A broader candidate conservation-priority area derived from the Zonation performance curve and the proportion of total suitability retained above the selected habitat-suitability threshold. It is reported only when it encompasses a larger area than the primary priority area.
Sensitivity
The proportion of occurrence locations correctly classified as suitable at a specified habitat-suitability threshold.
Specificity
The proportion of background locations correctly classified below a specified habitat-suitability threshold. In presence-background modelling, specificity refers to discrimination against sampled background rather than confirmed species absences.
Spatial autocorrelation
The tendency for environmental conditions or observations at nearby locations to be more similar than those farther apart. Its estimated range is used to inform the spatial scale of data partitioning and model evaluation.
Target-group approach
A sampling-bias correction method in which occurrence records from a group of similarly surveyed species are used to approximate spatial variation in recording effort. In this project, target-group information can additionally be weighted according to temporal overlap in recording activity.
Threshold
A selected value of continuous habitat suitability used to distinguish areas classified as suitable from those classified as unsuitable, or to calculate threshold-dependent evaluation statistics. Different threshold-selection criteria are reported because they serve different purposes.
Zonation / Zonation 5
Spatial conservation-prioritisation software used to rank landscape cells according to their contribution to conservation objectives. In this project, Zonation is applied separately to each species before subsequent multi-species prioritisation.
CAZMAX
The Zonation marginal-loss rule used for the single-species prioritisation analyses in this book. It determines how the conservation value lost when cells are removed contributes to the hierarchical ranking of sites.
